# Manuscript Research and Writing Blueprint

## Project Goal

Develop a review article for **Oral Oncology** on cisplatin use, toxicity mechanisms, and chronotherapy in oral squamous cell carcinoma (OSCC) and head-and-neck squamous cell carcinoma (HNSCC).

The manuscript should critically evaluate whether cisplatin toxicity in OSCC/HNSCC can be understood as an organ-specific, pathway-specific, and potentially time-dependent biological problem, and whether chronotype-aware cisplatin scheduling could be tested as a toxicity-sparing strategy.

---

# Target Journal

## Primary Target

**Oral Oncology**

## Article Type

**Review Article / Targeted Mini-Review**

## Journal Scope Fit

This manuscript is designed for **Oral Oncology** because it focuses on oral cancer and head-and-neck squamous cell carcinoma, and evaluates a clinically relevant therapeutic question: whether cisplatin toxicity can be reduced by integrating organ-specific toxicity mechanisms, current treatment logistics, and circadian biology.

The manuscript should be framed as a **critical review with translational perspective**, not as a systematic review, not as a meta-analysis, and not as a general cisplatin pharmacology review.

## Relevant Author Guideline Requirements

Oral Oncology welcomes review articles that are topical and provide a critical assessment of aspects of head-and-neck oncology. Review articles should collate, describe, and evaluate prior publications, accompanied by critical analysis leading to rational conclusions.

# Working Title

**Cisplatin Timing in Oral and Head-and-Neck Cancer: Organ-Specific Toxicity Mechanisms, Clinical Delivery Constraints, and the Rationale for Chronotherapy**

## Alternative Titles

1. **Toward Toxicity-Sparing Cisplatin Therapy in Oral Cancer: Integrating Organ Injury Mechanisms, Treatment Logistics, and Circadian Timing**
2. **Chronotype-Aware Cisplatin Scheduling in Oral and Head-and-Neck Cancer: Mechanistic Rationale and Translational Roadmap**
3. **Biological Time as a Modifiable Dimension of Cisplatin Therapy in Oral and Head-and-Neck Squamous Cell Carcinoma**

# Central Thesis

Cisplatin toxicity in OSCC/HNSCC should not be viewed only as a dose-dependent adverse-effect profile. It should be understood as an organ-specific, pathway-specific, and potentially time-dependent biological phenomenon.

Because several key determinants of cisplatin toxicity — DNA repair, platinum uptake, oxidative stress, mucosal proliferation, renal injury pathways, and inflammatory responses — are influenced by circadian biology, chronotype-aware cisplatin scheduling should be evaluated as a testable toxicity-sparing strategy.

---

# Core Argument

The manuscript should proceed through this logic:

1. **Cisplatin remains clinically central in OSCC/HNSCC.**
2. **Its clinical utility is limited by cumulative toxicity**, especially nephrotoxicity, ototoxicity, mucositis, nausea/vomiting, neuropathy, and treatment interruption.
3. **These toxicities are mechanistically explainable**, not merely descriptive adverse events.
4. **Many relevant toxicity mechanisms are linked to circadian-regulated pathways**, including XPA/NER, OCT2, BMAL1/ARNTL, glutathione/redox regulation, mucosal epithelial proliferation, DNA damage response, and inflammatory rhythms.
5. **Existing cisplatin chronotherapy evidence is promising but preliminary**, especially in OSCC and nasopharyngeal carcinoma.
6. **The next step should not be immediate clinical implementation**, but a chronotype-aware, biomarker-informed, toxicity-endpoint-driven clinical study.

---

# Section 2. Cisplatin chemistry and DNA damage mechanism

## Proposed title

**From platinum chemistry to DNA damage: why cisplatin creates a timing-sensitive injury problem**

## Target length

500–650 words.

## Purpose

Give readers enough mechanistic foundation to understand later toxicity and chronotherapy sections.

## Core content

### 2.1 Chemical structure

Cover briefly:

- Cisplatin is a square-planar platinum(II) compound.
- Its cis configuration is essential for DNA-crosslinking activity.
- Chloride ligand displacement generates reactive aqua species.
- These reactive platinum species bind nucleophilic sites, especially DNA purines.

### 2.2 Cellular uptake and accumulation

Cover:

- CTR1 as a major cisplatin influx route.
- OCT2 as especially relevant in renal proximal tubular cells and cochlear cells.
- ATP7A/ATP7B as efflux/sequestration transporters.
- The balance of uptake, detoxification, repair, and efflux shapes both efficacy and toxicity.

### 2.3 DNA adduct formation

Cover:

- 1,2-intrastrand d(GpG) crosslinks as the dominant adduct.
- 1,2-d(ApG) and 1,3-intrastrand adducts.
- DNA helix distortion.
- Recognition by DNA damage response machinery.

### 2.4 NER and apoptosis

Cover:

- Nucleotide excision repair removes bulky platinum adducts.
- XPA is a key verification/scaffold factor in NER.
- Persistent damage activates ATR/ATM → CHK1/CHK2 → p53.
- p53 activates PUMA/BAX-mediated mitochondrial apoptosis.

## Why this matters for the article

End this section by linking basic mechanism to the main thesis:

> Because cisplatin-induced injury depends on platinum uptake, DNA adduct burden, repair capacity, oxidative injury, inflammatory amplification, and apoptosis, rhythmic variation in these processes could alter toxicity at a given dose.

# Section 3. Cisplatin use in OSCC/HNSCC treatment pathways

## Proposed title

**Where cisplatin enters the OSCC/HNSCC treatment pathway: clinical decision points and timing constraints**

## Target length

700–900 words.

## Purpose

This section should not be a country-by-country guideline review. Instead, it should explain where cisplatin is used in OSCC/HNSCC and why these treatment settings create both opportunities and barriers for chronotherapy.

## Core framing

OSCC staging is internationally standardized, but cisplatin use is not determined by stage alone. It depends on treatment intent, resectability, pathological risk features, performance status, renal function, hearing status, neuropathy risk, and institutional workflow.

## Key argument

> The most useful way to discuss cisplatin use is not by country, but by clinical decision point.

## Subsections

### 3.1 OSCC staging and treatment intent

Explain:

- Early-stage OSCC is often treated with surgery or radiotherapy.
- Locally advanced OSCC/HNSCC often requires multimodality treatment.
- Cisplatin enters when systemic radiosensitisation, postoperative intensification, induction treatment, or systemic disease control is required.
- Cisplatin is therefore not tied to one stage alone.

### Suggested wording

> Because OSCC staging is internationally standardized, it provides a useful entry point for discussing cisplatin use. However, cisplatin administration is not dictated by stage alone. Rather, it is determined by treatment intent and risk features: definitive chemoradiotherapy in unresectable or organ-preservation contexts, postoperative chemoradiotherapy for high-risk pathological disease, induction chemotherapy in selected locally advanced cases, and systemic therapy for recurrent or metastatic disease.

### 3.2 Definitive concurrent chemoradiotherapy

Cover:

- Cisplatin acts as systemic therapy and radiosensitiser.
- Timing is constrained by daily radiotherapy schedules.
- Hydration, antiemetics, infusion-unit chair time, and renal monitoring matter.
- This setting is relevant for chronotherapy because treatment is scheduled and repeatable.

### 3.3 Postoperative high-risk chemoradiotherapy

Cover:

- Cisplatin may be used after surgery when high-risk pathological features are present.
- Patients may have postoperative nutritional compromise, wound recovery, pain, altered sleep, feeding tube use, and repeated hospital visits.
- Circadian disruption may already be present in this setting.

### 3.4 Induction chemotherapy

Cover:

- Cisplatin may be used as part of TPF/DCF-like regimens in selected locally advanced cases.
- Multi-drug regimens complicate chronotherapy because docetaxel and 5-FU also have pharmacokinetic and toxicity profiles.
- Cisplatin timing cannot be optimized in isolation.

### 3.5 Recurrent/metastatic systemic therapy

Cover:

- Cisplatin or platinum-containing therapy may be used in selected recurrent/metastatic patients.
- Regimens are heterogeneous.
- Chronotherapy may be harder to standardize in this setting.

### 3.6 Institutional variation as a chronotherapy barrier

Discuss variation as logistics, not as anecdotal national practice.

Important sources of variation:

- q3-week high-dose cisplatin vs weekly cisplatin.
- Inpatient vs outpatient administration.
- Hydration duration.
- Antiemetic timing.
- Dexamethasone timing.
- Radiotherapy appointment time.
- Nursing and infusion-suite capacity.
- Renal, electrolyte, auditory, and neurological monitoring.
- Patient transportation and evening infusion feasibility.

## Suggested closing sentence

> Current cisplatin protocols are largely biological-time blind: they specify dose, cycle, hydration, and monitoring, but rarely define when cisplatin should be administered relative to the patient’s endogenous circadian phase.

# Section 4. Organ-specific mechanisms of cisplatin toxicity

## Proposed title

**Why cisplatin injures specific organs: tissue uptake, DNA damage, oxidative stress, and repair failure**

## Target length

1,200–1,400 words.

## Purpose

This should be one of the strongest sections. The goal is to move beyond “cisplatin causes nephrotoxicity, ototoxicity, and mucositis” and explain why these toxicities occur.

## Core thesis

> Cisplatin toxicity is not a nonspecific adverse-effect burden. It reflects tissue-specific interactions among platinum uptake, DNA adduct formation, oxidative stress, mitochondrial dysfunction, inflammation, repair capacity, and cell-renewal kinetics.

## Subsections

### 4.1 Nephrotoxicity

Key points:

- Dose-limiting toxicity.
- Proximal tubular cells, especially S3 segment, are vulnerable.
- OCT2-mediated uptake contributes to platinum accumulation.
- Injury mechanisms:
  - DNA damage,
  - ROS,
  - mitochondrial injury,
  - p53–PUMA–BAX apoptosis,
  - inflammation,
  - tubular injury biomarkers.
- Clinical consequences:
  - creatinine elevation,
  - reduced GFR,
  - treatment delay,
  - cisplatin ineligibility.

### 4.2 Ototoxicity

Key points:

- Cochlear outer hair cells and stria vascularis are vulnerable.
- OCT2-mediated accumulation is mechanistically relevant.
- Injury is driven by oxidative stress, mitochondrial dysfunction, and hair-cell apoptosis.
- Often irreversible.
- Clinically important in survivorship.

### 4.3 Oral and gastrointestinal mucositis

Key points:

- Especially important in OSCC/HNSCC because radiotherapy and cisplatin interact.
- Rapidly proliferating mucosal basal epithelium is vulnerable.
- Injury mechanisms:
  - DNA damage,
  - ROS,
  - inflammatory amplification,
  - epithelial barrier breakdown,
  - ulceration,
  - microbiome and secondary inflammation.
- Clinical consequences:
  - pain,
  - opioid use,
  - feeding difficulty,
  - weight loss,
  - feeding tube placement,
  - treatment interruption.

### 4.4 Nausea and vomiting

Key points:

- Cisplatin is highly emetogenic.
- Mechanisms involve GI enterochromaffin cells, serotonin release, vagal afferents, central emetic pathways, and substance P/NK1 signaling.
- Modern antiemetics reduce but do not eliminate the burden.
- Chronotherapy studies often report GI toxicity endpoints.

### 4.5 Peripheral neuropathy

Key points:

- Dorsal root ganglia are vulnerable because of platinum accumulation and limited blood-nerve barrier protection.
- Injury mechanisms:
  - mitochondrial dysfunction,
  - oxidative stress,
  - axonal injury,
  - sensory neuron apoptosis.
- Long-term survivorship issue.

### 4.6 Myelosuppression

Key points:

- Less central than mucositis and nephrotoxicity for cisplatin, but relevant in combination regimens.
- Bone marrow progenitor proliferation may have circadian variation.
- Include briefly.

# Section 5. Circadian regulation of cisplatin pharmacology

## Proposed title

**Circadian molecular nodes linking cisplatin exposure to toxicity and repair**

## Target length

900–1,100 words.

## Purpose

This section should synthesize proteins and pathways that connect circadian biology to cisplatin toxicity.

## Main argument

> Cisplatin chronotherapy is biologically plausible because multiple determinants of cisplatin toxicity and repair are circadian-regulated or clock-associated.

## Molecular nodes to include

### 5.1 XPA / NER

This should be the central node.

Cover:

- XPA is central to nucleotide excision repair.
- NER removes bulky platinum-DNA adducts.
- XPA oscillation provides the strongest mechanistic link between circadian biology and cisplatin DNA damage repair.
- Normal tissue repair may be higher at specific circadian phases.
- Key knowledge gap: XPA rhythm has not been adequately demonstrated in OSCC tumor tissue or oral mucosa.

### 5.2 OCT2 / SLC22A2

Cover:

- OCT2 mediates cisplatin uptake in renal proximal tubular cells and cochlear cells.
- OCT2 is central to nephrotoxicity and ototoxicity.
- Rhythmic expression or clock-linked regulation could create timing-dependent platinum uptake.
- More direct evidence in HNSCC-relevant settings is needed.

### 5.3 BMAL1 / ARNTL

Cover:

- Core clock transcription factor.
- Tissue-specific effects.
- In kidney, BMAL1 may facilitate cisplatin injury.
- In nasopharyngeal carcinoma, ARNTL silencing may contribute to cisplatin resistance.
- This creates a “BMAL1 paradox”: clock biology may simultaneously shape host toxicity and tumor response.

### 5.4 GSH / redox metabolism

Cover:

- Glutathione detoxifies reactive platinum species.
- GSH biosynthesis and redox capacity may be rhythmic.
- Increased detoxification in normal tissue could reduce toxicity.
- However, increased detoxification in tumors could reduce efficacy.
- Evidence in OSCC/HNSCC remains incomplete.

### 5.5 Mucosal proliferation and cell cycle timing

Cover:

- Oral/GI mucosa has rhythmic proliferation.
- Timing cisplatin when mucosal progenitors are less proliferative may reduce mucositis.
- This is clinically relevant because mucositis is a major toxicity endpoint in HNSCC.

### 5.6 PER/CRY and clock integrity

Cover:

- PER/CRY are required for robust clock function.
- Clock disruption may abolish chronotherapy benefit.
- Hospitalisation, artificial light, dexamethasone, pain, opioids, and feeding schedule may disrupt patient rhythms.

# Section 6. Clinical evidence for cisplatin chronotherapy

## Proposed title

**Clinical and preclinical evidence: promising toxicity signals but no proven survival benefit**

## Target length

600–800 words.

## Purpose

Evaluate existing evidence critically.

## Main message

> The evidence supports toxicity reduction as the most defensible near-term endpoint. It does not yet support clinical implementation or survival benefit claims.

## Evidence to include

### 6.1 OSCC clinical pilot

Use the OSCC crossover pilot as the most directly relevant clinical evidence.

Interpretation:

- Direct OSCC evidence.
- Very small sample size.
- Hypothesis-generating.
- Useful for selecting toxicity endpoints.

### 6.2 NPC randomized phase II trial

Use the NPC randomized phase II trial as supporting evidence.

Interpretation:

- Larger clinical signal.
- NPC is not OSCC.
- EBV-associated biology and radiation field differ.
- Not powered for survival.
- Supports mucositis/GI toxicity endpoint selection.

### 6.3 Preclinical models

Include:

- Mouse models showing time-of-day differences in toxicity and DNA damage repair.
- Clock-disrupted models showing loss of chronotherapy benefit.
- These models support biological plausibility but are not HNSCC-specific.

### 6.4 Counter-evidence and limitations

Discuss:

- No OSCC/HNSCC randomized trial has demonstrated survival benefit.
- Existing trials are underpowered.
- Toxicity endpoints vary.
- Clock time and biological time are not equivalent.
- Modern antiemetic regimens may reduce the incremental value of timing for nausea/vomiting.
- Patient circadian disruption may confound fixed-time protocols.

# Section 7. Future direction

## Proposed title

**From fixed clock-time dosing to chronotype-aware cisplatin scheduling**

## Target length

700–900 words.

## Purpose

This is the translational payoff section. It should propose a concrete future research path.

## Main argument

> The next step is not routine evening cisplatin administration, but a biomarker-informed, chronotype-stratified clinical trial focused on toxicity reduction and treatment completion.

## Proposed study concept

### Trial name concept

**Chronotype-informed cisplatin scheduling for toxicity reduction in OSCC/HNSCC receiving chemoradiotherapy**

## Proposed trial design

| Element | Proposed design |
|---|---|
| Population | Locally advanced OSCC/HNSCC receiving definitive or postoperative cisplatin-RT |
| Regimen | Weekly cisplatin 40 mg/m² may be more logistically feasible than q3-week high-dose cisplatin |
| Comparator | Standard institutional timing vs chronotype-adjusted cisplatin timing |
| Intervention | Cisplatin scheduled to patient-specific late biological afternoon/evening phase |
| Stratification | Chronotype, baseline rhythm robustness, inpatient/outpatient status, dexamethasone timing |
| Primary endpoint | Grade ≥3 mucositis or nephrotoxicity |
| Secondary endpoints | nausea/vomiting, ototoxicity, neuropathy, cumulative cisplatin dose, RT interruptions, feeding tube use, QoL |
| Exploratory endpoints | XPA rhythm, OCT2-related renal injury markers, BMAL1/ARNTL expression or methylation, actigraphy rhythm robustness |
| Monitoring tools | actigraphy, sleep diary, Munich Chronotype Questionnaire, optional DLMO, wearable temperature/heart-rate monitoring |
| Implementation outcomes | infusion feasibility, patient adherence, nursing burden, treatment delays |

## Key design principles

### 1. Chronotype-aware, not fixed-clock

Do not assume that 16:00 or 19:30 is optimal for everyone.

### 2. Toxicity-first endpoints

The first trial should focus on:

- grade ≥3 mucositis,
- nephrotoxicity,
- nausea/vomiting,
- treatment interruption,
- cumulative cisplatin completion.

Do not use survival as the primary endpoint in an early study.

### 3. Circadian integrity assessment

Patients undergoing CRT may have disrupted rhythms from:

- pain,
- mucositis,
- opioids,
- tube feeding,
- hospital lighting,
- sleep fragmentation,
- dexamethasone,
- anxiety,
- malnutrition.

### 4. Biomarker integration

Potential biomarkers:

- PBMC XPA rhythm.
- Oral mucosa XPA expression.
- Urinary kidney injury markers.
- OCT2-related transporter markers.
- BMAL1/ARNTL expression or methylation.
- Rest-activity rhythm robustness.

### 5. Workflow feasibility

Clinical implementation depends on:

- infusion unit hours,
- hydration time,
- RT scheduling,
- antiemetic timing,
- nursing availability,
- patient transportation,
- outpatient vs inpatient workflow.

# Section 8. Conclusion

## Target length

250–350 words.

## Purpose

End with a balanced, rational conclusion.

## Must say

- Cisplatin remains important in OSCC/HNSCC.
- Toxicity limits treatment completion.
- Toxicity is mechanistically organ-specific.
- Circadian biology plausibly modifies several key toxicity pathways.
- Existing evidence supports further study but not immediate clinical implementation.
- Future studies should integrate chronotype, circadian monitoring, toxicity endpoints, and clinical workflow.

## Suggested conclusion paragraph

> Cisplatin remains indispensable in the management of OSCC and HNSCC, but its clinical value is constrained by organ-specific toxicities that reflect defined mechanisms of platinum uptake, DNA damage, oxidative stress, repair failure, inflammation, and apoptosis. Circadian regulation of DNA repair, transporter expression, redox capacity, and mucosal proliferation provides a biologically plausible rationale for toxicity-sparing cisplatin scheduling. However, current evidence remains preliminary, with clinical signals largely restricted to toxicity reduction rather than survival improvement. The field should therefore move from fixed clock-time chronotherapy toward chronotype-aware, biomarker-informed trial designs that account for patient circadian integrity and real-world treatment logistics. Such studies could determine whether biological time is a modifiable dimension of cisplatin therapy in oral and head-and-neck cancer.

# Claims Calibration

## Claims that are safe

- Cisplatin remains important in OSCC/HNSCC.
- Cisplatin toxicities are organ-specific and mechanistically explainable.
- XPA/NER provides a plausible mechanistic link between circadian biology and cisplatin DNA repair.
- OCT2 is relevant to renal and cochlear cisplatin accumulation.
- BMAL1 may have tissue-specific effects in cisplatin response.
- Existing chronotherapy evidence is hypothesis-generating.
- Toxicity reduction is a more defensible endpoint than survival benefit.
- Future trials should consider chronotype and circadian integrity.

## Claims to avoid

- Evening cisplatin is universally optimal.
- Cisplatin chronotherapy is ready for routine clinical use.
- Chronotherapy improves survival in OSCC/HNSCC.
- OSCC tumor XPA rhythm is already proven.
- Hospital or country-specific cisplatin practice can be generalized without evidence.
- Chronotherapy will reduce all cisplatin toxicities equally.
