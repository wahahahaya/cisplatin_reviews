# Cisplatin Chronotherapy in Oral and Head-and-Neck Cancer

## Proposal Overview

This project develops a focused review article for **Oral Oncology** on cisplatin use, organ-specific toxicity mechanisms, and chronotherapy in oral squamous cell carcinoma (OSCC) and head-and-neck squamous cell carcinoma (HNSCC).

The central premise is that cisplatin toxicity should not be viewed only as a dose-dependent adverse-effect profile. Instead, cisplatin toxicity may be understood as an organ-specific, pathway-specific, and potentially time-dependent biological phenomenon. Because key determinants of cisplatin toxicity—including platinum uptake, DNA repair, redox buffering, mucosal proliferation, inflammation, mitochondrial injury, and apoptotic threshold—are influenced by circadian biology, chronotype-aware cisplatin scheduling may be a testable strategy to reduce treatment-limiting toxicity.

This project is not intended to advocate immediate clinical implementation of cisplatin chronotherapy. The goal is to synthesize the mechanistic and clinical rationale for future biomarker-informed, chronotype-aware, toxicity-endpoint-driven trials.

## Central Thesis

Cisplatin remains clinically central in OSCC/HNSCC, but its utility is limited by cumulative toxicity. These toxicities are not nonspecific adverse effects; they arise from defined organ-specific interactions among platinum uptake, DNA adduct formation, DNA repair, oxidative stress, mitochondrial injury, inflammation, epithelial turnover, and apoptosis.

If these injury determinants vary across circadian time, then the same cisplatin exposure may generate different toxicity profiles depending on when it is administered. This creates a mechanistic rationale for evaluating chronotype-aware cisplatin scheduling as a toxicity-sparing strategy.

## Core Research Questions

1. Where does cisplatin enter OSCC/HNSCC treatment pathways?
2. Which clinical delivery settings are most relevant for future timing interventions?
3. Why does cisplatin injure specific organs such as kidney, cochlea, oral/GI mucosa, and peripheral nerves?
4. Which molecular and cellular processes create potential circadian “timing gates” for cisplatin toxicity?
5. What clinical and preclinical evidence currently supports cisplatin chronotherapy?
6. What future trial design would be most appropriate for testing chronotype-aware cisplatin scheduling?

## Manuscript Logic

The manuscript proceeds through the following argument:

1. Cisplatin remains a backbone of OSCC/HNSCC treatment.
2. Its clinical value is constrained by nephrotoxicity, ototoxicity, mucositis, nausea/vomiting, neuropathy, cumulative dose limitation, and treatment interruption.
3. These toxicities are mechanistically explainable and organ-specific.
4. The biological processes that drive cisplatin toxicity may vary across circadian time.
5. Circadian timing gates such as XPA/NER, OCT2, GSH/redox buffering, mucosal proliferation, BMAL1/ARNTL, and PER/CRY provide a plausible mechanistic basis for chronotherapy.
6. Existing clinical evidence is promising but preliminary and should not be interpreted as practice-changing.
7. Future studies should test chronotype-aware scheduling with toxicity-first endpoints, circadian monitoring, and workflow feasibility assessment.

## Proposed Manuscript Structure

### 1. Introduction: Cisplatin toxicity as a biological-time problem in OSCC/HNSCC

This section introduces cisplatin as a central cytotoxic and radiosensitising agent in OSCC/HNSCC and frames toxicity as the key barrier to treatment completion.

### 2. From platinum chemistry to DNA damage

This section summarizes cisplatin structure, aquation, cellular uptake, DNA adduct formation, nucleotide excision repair, and apoptosis. It provides the mechanistic foundation for later toxicity and timing sections.

### 3. Cisplatin use in OSCC/HNSCC treatment pathways

This section explains where cisplatin enters OSCC/HNSCC treatment pathways. It is not a country-by-country guideline review, but it does acknowledge real-world practice patterns that affect timing feasibility: surgery-first oral cavity management, postoperative high-risk chemoradiotherapy, definitive chemoradiotherapy, induction chemotherapy, recurrent/metastatic systemic therapy, weekly versus three-weekly dosing, and supportive-care logistics.

### 4. Organ-specific mechanisms of cisplatin toxicity

This section explains why cisplatin injures specific organs. It covers nephrotoxicity, ototoxicity, oral/GI mucositis, nausea/vomiting, peripheral neuropathy, and myelosuppression. It ends with a bridging subsection that reframes these organ toxicities as potentially timing-sensitive vulnerabilities.

### 5. Circadian timing gates in cisplatin uptake, repair, redox control, and tissue vulnerability

This section explains how the toxicity mechanisms described in Section 4 may become timing-sensitive. Key timing gates include XPA/NER, OCT2/SLC22A2, GSH/redox buffering, mucosal proliferation, BMAL1/ARNTL, and PER/CRY-mediated circadian integrity.

### 6. Clinical and preclinical evidence for cisplatin chronotherapy

This section evaluates available evidence, including OSCC pilot data, nasopharyngeal carcinoma trials, and preclinical models. The emphasis is that toxicity reduction is the most defensible near-term endpoint, while survival benefit remains unproven.

### 7. From fixed clock-time dosing to chronotype-aware cisplatin scheduling

This section proposes a future clinical trial framework based on chronotype, circadian rhythm robustness, toxicity endpoints, biomarkers, and real-world treatment logistics.

### 8. Conclusion

The conclusion should remain balanced: cisplatin chronotherapy is biologically plausible and clinically testable, but not ready for routine clinical implementation.

## Claims Calibration

### Acceptable claims

- Cisplatin remains important in OSCC/HNSCC.
- Cisplatin toxicities are organ-specific and mechanistically explainable.
- XPA/NER provides a plausible mechanistic link between circadian biology and cisplatin DNA repair.
- OCT2 is relevant to renal and cochlear cisplatin accumulation.
- BMAL1/ARNTL may have tissue-specific effects in cisplatin response.
- Existing cisplatin chronotherapy evidence is hypothesis-generating.
- Toxicity reduction is a more defensible early endpoint than survival benefit.
- Future trials should consider chronotype, circadian integrity, biomarkers, and clinical workflow.

### Claims to avoid

- Evening cisplatin is universally optimal.
- Cisplatin chronotherapy is ready for routine clinical use.
- Chronotherapy improves survival in OSCC/HNSCC.
- OSCC tumor XPA rhythm is already proven.
- Hospital-specific or country-specific cisplatin practice can be generalized without supporting evidence.
- Chronotherapy will reduce all cisplatin toxicities equally.

## Proposed Future Trial Concept

**Chronotype-informed cisplatin scheduling for toxicity reduction in OSCC/HNSCC receiving chemoradiotherapy**

| Element | Proposed design |
|---|---|
| Population | Locally advanced OSCC/HNSCC receiving definitive or postoperative cisplatin-RT |
| Regimen | Weekly cisplatin 40 mg/m² may be more feasible than q3-week high-dose cisplatin for repeated timing interventions |
| Comparator | Standard institutional timing versus chronotype-adjusted cisplatin timing |
| Primary endpoint | Grade ≥3 mucositis or nephrotoxicity |
| Secondary endpoints | Nausea/vomiting, ototoxicity, neuropathy, cumulative cisplatin dose, RT interruptions, feeding tube use, QoL |
| Exploratory endpoints | XPA rhythm, OCT2-related renal injury markers, BMAL1/ARNTL expression or methylation, actigraphy rhythm robustness |
| Monitoring tools | Actigraphy, sleep diary, chronotype questionnaire, optional DLMO, wearable temperature/heart-rate monitoring |
| Implementation outcomes | Infusion feasibility, patient adherence, nursing burden, treatment delays |
