## Landmark Trials

### 1. Bernier J, et al. (2004) — EORTC Trial 22931
**Citation:** Bernier J, Domenge C, Ozsahin M, et al. "Postoperative irradiation with or without concomitant chemotherapy for locally advanced head and neck cancer." *N Engl J Med.* 2004;350(19):1945–1952. DOI: 10.1056/NEJMoa032641. PMID: 15128894.

**Extraction:**
- **Setting:** Postoperative (adjuvant) CRT; curatively-intended surgery followed by adjuvant chemoradiotherapy for stage III–IV HNSCC.
- **Cisplatin dose/schedule:** 100 mg/m² IV on days 1, 22, and 43 of the RT course (q3-weekly, 3 cycles); concurrent with 66 Gy over 6.5 weeks.
- **Population:** 334 patients (167 RT alone vs. 167 cisplatin + RT); HNSCC subsites included oral cavity, oropharynx, larynx, hypopharynx; OSCC % not separately reported [verify exact subsite breakdown]. Stage III–IV.
- **Key toxicity endpoints:** Grade 3–4 acute mucosal toxicity higher in combined arm; late toxicity (xerostomia, dysphagia) similar between arms; renal toxicity not cited as dose-limiting in original report. Treatment-related deaths: 3% combined arm vs. 1% RT alone [verify].
- **Efficacy:** Combined chemoradiotherapy significantly improved progression-free survival (HR ~0.75) and locoregional control vs. RT alone. 5-year OS improved (~53% vs. ~40% [verify exact figures from full text]). This was a positive trial for OS — in contrast to concurrent RTOG 9501.
- **Treatment completion:** 82% of patients completed all three cisplatin cycles [verify]; treatment compliance reported as acceptable with no undue late toxicity.
- **Chronotherapy relevance:** Treatment was entirely biological-time blind. Cisplatin was administered on fixed calendar days (days 1, 22, 43) dictated by the RT course structure, with no regard for circadian phase, time-of-day, or patient chronotype. Daily fractionated RT constrains cisplatin timing to weekday slots driven by linac scheduling rather than biology.

### 2. Cooper JS, et al. (2004) — RTOG 9501
**Citation:** Cooper JS, Pajak TF, Forastiere AA, et al. "Postoperative concurrent radiotherapy and chemotherapy for high-risk squamous-cell carcinoma of the head and neck." *N Engl J Med.* 2004;350(19):1937–1944. DOI: 10.1056/NEJMoa032646. PMID: 15128893.

**Extraction:**
- **Setting:** Postoperative CRT for high-risk HNSCC (positive margins, extracapsular nodal extension, >1 positive lymph node, perineural invasion, vascular invasion, or involved mucosa). Enrolled September 1995 – April 2000.
- **Cisplatin dose/schedule:** 100 mg/m² IV on days 1, 22, and 43 of RT (q3-weekly); concurrent with 60–66 Gy in 30–33 fractions over ~6.5 weeks.
- **Population:** 459 patients; 231 RT alone, 228 RT + cisplatin. HNSCC subsites: oral cavity, oropharynx, larynx, hypopharynx. OSCC proportion not separately extracted here [verify from publication Table 1].
- **Key toxicity endpoints:** Grade 3–4 hematologic and non-hematologic toxicity significantly more common in combined arm. Mucositis, nausea/vomiting reported. Renal toxicity not highlighted as primary dose-limiter. Completion of all 3 cisplatin cycles: ~61% [verify — commonly cited in subsequent commentary].
- **Efficacy:** Significantly lower local/regional failure (HR 0.61; 95% CI: 0.41–0.91; P = 0.01) and improved disease-free survival in combined arm. Importantly, OS was not significantly improved in this trial (P = 0.19) — in contrast to EORTC 22931. Post-hoc combined EORTC/RTOG analysis confirmed benefit concentrated in patients with positive margins or extracapsular extension.
- **Treatment completion:** ~61% completed all planned cisplatin cycles; treatment interruptions due to toxicity were not uncommon.
- **Chronotherapy relevance:** Same biological-time-blind design as EORTC 22931. q3-weekly dosing tied to RT course calendar; no circadian considerations documented. Daily RT delivery (5 days/week) imposes the primary logistical constraint on cisplatin scheduling.

### 3. Forastiere AA, et al. (2003) — RTOG 91-11
**Citation:** Forastiere AA, Goepfert H, Maor M, et al. "Concurrent chemotherapy and radiotherapy for organ preservation in advanced laryngeal cancer." *N Engl J Med.* 2003;349(22):2091–2098. DOI: 10.1056/NEJMoa031317. PMID: 14645636.

**Extraction:**
- **Setting:** Definitive (organ-preservation) CRT for stage III–IV laryngeal cancer (glottic or supraglottic). Three-arm: concurrent cisplatin + RT vs. induction PF → RT vs. RT alone.
- **Cisplatin dose/schedule:** Concurrent arm: cisplatin 100 mg/m² IV on days 1, 22, 43 concurrent with 70 Gy in 35 fractions. Induction arm: cisplatin 100 mg/m²/day x1 + 5-FU 1000 mg/m²/day x4 (2-hour infusion, q3-weekly x3) → RT alone.
- **Population:** ~520 patients with advanced laryngeal squamous cell carcinoma (T2–T4, N0–N2). Primary site: larynx only — OSCC not represented; this trial is included for cisplatin CRT scheduling paradigm.
- **Key toxicity endpoints:** Grade 3–4 toxicities higher in concurrent arm (mucositis, neutropenia). Acute toxicity was manageable but substantial. Late toxicity (dysphagia, laryngeal dysfunction) reported at extended follow-up.
- **Efficacy:** At 2 years, laryngectomy-free survival: concurrent RT+cisplatin 70.4% vs. induction PF→RT 64.3% vs. RT alone 59.2%. Concurrent arm superior for larynx preservation. 2-year OS similar across arms (~75%). Long-term follow-up (2006 update) confirmed locoregional control advantage for concurrent arm; 10-year data showed no OS detriment to organ-preservation strategy.
- **Treatment completion:** Concurrent arm completion data: approximately 82% received ≥2 cycles of cisplatin [verify from long-term follow-up data].
- **Chronotherapy relevance:** Definitively time-agnostic: cisplatin administered on fixed RT fractionation days (days 1, 22, 43) with no circadian scheduling rationale. The daily RT regimen (70 Gy/35 fractions, weekdays only) hard-constrains when cisplatin can be delivered — a scheduling architecture that predates and ignores chronobiology.

### 4. Noronha V, et al. (2018) — Tata Memorial Phase III Trial
**Citation:** Noronha V, Joshi A, Patil VM, et al. "Once-a-week versus once-every-3-weeks cisplatin chemoradiation for locally advanced head and neck cancer: A phase III randomized noninferiority trial." *J Clin Oncol.* 2018;36(11):1064–1072. DOI: 10.1200/JCO.2017.74.9457. PMID: 29220295.

**Extraction:**
- **Setting:** Predominantly postoperative CRT (93% adjuvant for high-risk features post-surgery); 7% definitive CRT. Curative intent. Single-centre (Tata Memorial Centre, Mumbai).
- **Cisplatin dose/schedule:** Weekly arm: 30 mg/m² IV once weekly (×6–7 cycles); q3-weekly arm: 100 mg/m² IV on days 1, 22, 43. Both concurrent with RT (60–66 Gy).
- **Population:** 300 patients randomized (150 per arm); 89% male; **87% oral cavity cancers (OSCC)** — notably high OSCC representation; HNSCC with high-risk features post-resection. Indian population (high tobacco/betel nut exposure context).
- **Key toxicity endpoints:** Severe (grade ≥3) adverse events more common in q3-weekly arm: nephrotoxicity, ototoxicity, nausea/vomiting significantly higher. Weekly arm had better toxicity profile. Grade ≥3 nephrotoxicity significantly lower with weekly schedule [verify exact ORs from full text].
- **Efficacy:** Primary endpoint (locoregional control at 2 years): q3-weekly arm 79.4% vs. weekly arm 64.9%; absolute difference 14.6% (95% CI: 5.7%–23.5%). Upper bound exceeded 15% noninferiority margin — weekly cisplatin did NOT demonstrate noninferiority. Median PFS: q3-weekly 28.6 months vs. weekly 17.7 months (HR = 1.24, P = 0.21). Median OS: not reached (q3-weekly) vs. 39.5 months (weekly) (HR = 1.14, P = 0.48).
- **Treatment completion:** Weekly arm: higher proportion received all planned cycles (88% vs. 71% in q3-weekly arm). Despite better completion, efficacy was inferior.
- **Chronotherapy relevance:** All cisplatin was administered in the morning during standard institutional hours, concurrent with fixed-schedule RT (weekday only). No circadian timing considered. The trial's 87% OSCC cohort makes it the most directly relevant trial for oral cancer chronotherapy review — yet scheduling was entirely logistics-driven.

### 5. Bonner JA, et al. (2006) — Non-platinum Comparator (Cetuximab)
**Citation:** Bonner JA, Harari PM, Giralt J, et al. "Radiotherapy plus cetuximab for squamous-cell carcinoma of the head and neck." *N Engl J Med.* 2006;354(6):567–578. DOI: 10.1056/NEJMoa053422. PMID: 16467544.

**Extraction:**
- **Setting:** Definitive RT + concurrent targeted therapy (EGFR inhibitor) for locoregionally advanced HNSCC. Head-to-head vs. RT alone (not vs. cisplatin + RT).
- **Cisplatin dose/schedule:** No cisplatin used. Cetuximab: 400 mg/m² IV loading dose 1 week before RT, then 250 mg/m² IV weekly x7 during RT. RT: 70–76.8 Gy.
- **Population:** 424 patients (211 cetuximab + RT; 213 RT alone). Sites: oropharynx (60%), larynx (25%), hypopharynx (15%). Low OSCC representation.
- **Key toxicity endpoints:** Acneiform rash (cetuximab-specific; 87% any grade, 17% grade 3); infusion reactions (3% grade 3–4). Mucositis similar between arms. No renal toxicity, ototoxicity, or neuropathy concerns — key advantage over cisplatin in selected patients.
- **Efficacy:** Median duration of locoregional control: 24.4 months (cetuximab) vs. 14.9 months (RT alone) (HR = 0.68; P = 0.005). Median OS: 49.0 months vs. 29.3 months (HR = 0.74; P = 0.03).
- **Treatment completion:** Not the primary comparison of interest here; high completion rate for weekly cetuximab.
- **Chronotherapy relevance:** Cited as non-platinum alternative for cisplatin-ineligible patients (renal impairment, hearing loss, poor PS). Weekly cetuximab is tied to daily RT logistics in the same biologically agnostic scheduling framework as cisplatin. Note: subsequent data (De Felice 2018; RTOG 1016; De-ESCALATE HPV) suggest cisplatin + RT is superior to cetuximab + RT for HPV+ oropharynx cancers; cetuximab use has narrowed considerably.

## Weekly vs. 3-Weekly Cisplatin Comparisons

### 6. Kiyota N, et al. (2022) — JCOG1008
**Citation:** Kiyota N, Tahara M, Mizusawa J, et al. "Weekly cisplatin plus radiation for postoperative head and neck cancer (JCOG1008): A multicenter, noninferiority, phase II/III randomized controlled trial." *J Clin Oncol.* 2022;40(9):938–946. DOI: 10.1200/JCO.21.01293. PMID: 35133872 [verify].

**Extraction:**
- **Setting:** Postoperative CRT for high-risk locally advanced HNSCC (squamous cell carcinoma). Multicenter Japanese trial.
- **Cisplatin dose/schedule:** Weekly arm: cisplatin 40 mg/m² IV once weekly; q3-weekly arm: cisplatin 100 mg/m² IV on days 1, 22, 43. Both concurrent with 60–66 Gy.
- **Population:** 261 patients enrolled (132 q3-weekly; 129 weekly). Japanese HNSCC patients; subsite distribution [verify from publication]; high-risk postoperative features (positive margins, extracapsular extension).
- **Key toxicity endpoints:** Weekly arm: significantly less grade ≥3 neutropenia (35% vs. 49%), less grade ≥3 infection (7% vs. 12%), less renal impairment, less hearing impairment vs. q3-weekly arm. Better overall toxicity profile with weekly schedule.
- **Efficacy:** Weekly cisplatin was NONINFERIOR to q3-weekly for OS: HR = 0.69 (99.1% CI: 0.374–1.273; P for noninferiority = 0.0027, margin <1.32). 2-year OS: 77.7% (weekly) vs. 74.2% (q3-weekly); 3-year OS: 71.6% vs. 59.1%.
- **Treatment completion:** [verify from full publication].
- **Chronotherapy relevance:** Demonstrates that cisplatin scheduling flexibility exists (weekly vs. q3-weekly achieves equivalent OS), yet neither arm incorporated any circadian timing rationale. The noninferiority finding opens space for chronotherapy optimization within the weekly paradigm.

### 7. Systematic Review: Weekly vs. 3-Weekly Cisplatin (Multiple Authors)
**Citation (representative):** Szturz P, et al. "Weekly low-dose versus three-weekly high-dose cisplatin for concurrent chemoradiation in locoregionally advanced non-nasopharyngeal head and neck cancer: a systematic review and meta-analysis of aggregate data." *Oncologist.* 2017;22(9):1056–1066. PMC: PMC5599190 [verify full citation details including DOI and PMID].

**Extraction:**
- **Setting:** Systematic review/meta-analysis of RCTs comparing weekly low-dose vs. q3-weekly high-dose cisplatin concurrent with definitive or postoperative RT in HNSCC.
- **Cisplatin dose/schedule:** Weekly: typically 30–40 mg/m²; q3-weekly: 100 mg/m².
- **Population:** Pooled HNSCC patients across multiple sites and trials.
- **Key toxicity findings:**
  - Grade ≥3 nephrotoxicity: significantly lower with weekly regimen.
  - Grade ≥3 leukopenia: significantly lower with weekly regimen (1% vs. 19%; P = 0.0083).
  - Grade ≥3 mucositis: no significant difference between arms (OR 1.10; 95% CI 0.79–1.52; P = 0.57).
  - Higher proportion of patients completing all planned cycles with weekly schedule (88% vs. 71%; P = 0.0017).
- **Efficacy:** Complete response rates: 63.2% (weekly) vs. 67.1% (q3-weekly) (P = 0.32); 2-year OS: 51.2% vs. 49.5% (P = 0.45). Broadly equivalent efficacy.
- **Treatment completion:** Weekly arm superior for compliance.
- **Chronotherapy relevance:** Meta-analysis confirms scheduling paradigm is flexible (weekly vs. q3-weekly) without compromising efficacy, reinforcing that the temporal architecture of cisplatin delivery can be modified — a prerequisite for chronotherapy-integrated trials.

## Postoperative High-Risk CRT

*(See Bernier 2004 [§1] and Cooper 2004 [§2] above. Also see JCOG1008 [§6] and Noronha 2018 [§4].)*

### 8. Bernier J, et al. (2005) — Pooled EORTC/RTOG Analysis
**Citation:** Bernier J, Cooper JS, Pajak TF, et al. "Defining risk levels in locally advanced head and neck cancers: a comparative analysis of concurrent postoperative radiation plus chemotherapy trials of the EORTC (#22931) and RTOG (#9501)." *Head Neck.* 2005;27(10):843–850. PMID: 16161069.

**Extraction:**
- **Setting:** Pooled analysis of EORTC 22931 and RTOG 9501 to define high-risk features warranting postoperative CRT.
- **Cisplatin dose/schedule:** As per constituent trials: 100 mg/m² q3-weekly x3 cycles concurrent with RT.
- **Population:** Combined 747 patients from both trials.
- **Key finding:** Benefit from adding cisplatin concentrated in patients with positive surgical margins and/or extracapsular extension. Patients with other risk factors (e.g., multiple positive nodes without ECE, PNI alone) showed no statistically significant OS benefit from adding cisplatin.
- **Chronotherapy relevance:** Biological-time-blind throughout; defines the patient-selection framework for cisplatin postoperative CRT that subsequent chronotherapy trials must respect.

## Induction Chemotherapy (TPF/DCF)

### 9. Vermorken JB, et al. (2007) — TAX 323 (EORTC 24971)
**Citation:** Vermorken JB, Remenar E, van Herpen C, et al. "Cisplatin, fluorouracil, and docetaxel in unresectable head and neck cancer." *N Engl J Med.* 2007;357(17):1695–1704. DOI: 10.1056/NEJMoa071028. PMID: 17960012.

**Extraction:**
- **Setting:** Induction chemotherapy (IC) for unresectable locoregionally advanced HNSCC, followed by radiotherapy alone. Phase III (EORTC 24971/TAX 323).
- **Cisplatin dose/schedule:** TPF arm: docetaxel 75 mg/m² day 1, cisplatin 75 mg/m² day 1, 5-FU 750 mg/m²/day continuous infusion days 1–5, q3-weekly x4 cycles → RT. PF arm: cisplatin 100 mg/m² day 1, 5-FU 1000 mg/m²/day CI days 1–5, q3-weekly x4 → RT.
- **Population:** 358 patients (TPF n=177, PF n=181); unresectable stage III–IV HNSCC; oropharynx, hypopharynx, larynx, oral cavity subsites [verify specific OSCC %].
- **Key toxicity endpoints:** TPF: less grade 3–4 neutropenia; significantly less grade 3–4 mucositis compared to PF. Grade 3–4 alopecia more common with TPF. Docetaxel-associated toxicities (fluid retention, neuropathy) added to cisplatin's usual profile.
- **Efficacy:** Median PFS: 11.0 months (TPF) vs. 8.2 months (PF); HR = 0.72 (P = 0.007). Median OS: 18.8 months (TPF) vs. 14.5 months (PF); HR = 0.73 (P = 0.02). Post-induction overall response: 59% (TPF) vs. 47% (PF).
- **Treatment completion:** [verify from publication; commonly cited that ≥3 cycles completed in majority of patients].
- **Chronotherapy relevance:** Induction IC is delivered in discrete q3-weekly cycles separate from RT — logistically distinct window where chrono-optimized cisplatin delivery is more feasible than during concurrent CRT, since there is no daily RT constraint. This makes IC the setting most amenable to circadian timing trials for cisplatin.

### 10. Posner MR, et al. (2007) — TAX 324
**Citation:** Posner MR, Hershock DM, Blajman CR, et al. "Cisplatin and fluorouracil alone or with docetaxel in head and neck cancer." *N Engl J Med.* 2007;357(17):1705–1715. DOI: 10.1056/NEJMoa070956. PMID: 17960013.

**Extraction:**
- **Setting:** Induction chemotherapy for locally advanced resectable/unresectable HNSCC, followed by concurrent carboplatin + RT (sequential combined modality). Phase III (TAX 324).
- **Cisplatin dose/schedule:** TPF arm: docetaxel 75 mg/m² day 1, cisplatin 100 mg/m² day 1, 5-FU 1000 mg/m²/day CI days 1–4, q3-weekly x3 → carboplatin + RT. PF arm: cisplatin 100 mg/m² day 1, 5-FU 1000 mg/m²/day CI days 1–5, q3-weekly x3 → carboplatin + RT.
- **Population:** 501 patients (TPF n=255, PF n=246); unresectable or requiring organ preservation, stage III–IV. Sites: oropharynx, larynx, hypopharynx, oral cavity [OSCC % verify].
- **Key toxicity endpoints:** Grade 3–4 neutropenia: 83% (TPF) vs. 56% (PF). Febrile neutropenia, mucositis, infection similar. Growth factor support used. TPF tolerability improved vs. PF overall.
- **Efficacy:** Median OS: 70.6 months (TPF) vs. 34.8 months (PF); significantly better with TPF. 3-year OS: 62% (TPF) vs. 48% (PF). 5-year OS: 52% (TPF) vs. 42% (PF) [from long-term follow-up publication, Langer 2014, Lancet Oncol].
- **Treatment completion:** [verify from publication].
- **Chronotherapy relevance:** Same as TAX 323 — induction-phase cisplatin delivered outside the daily RT tether, making this the most accessible setting for circadian scheduling interventions.

## Guidelines & Meta-Analyses

### 11. Pignon JP, et al. (2009) — MACH-NC Update
**Citation:** Pignon JP, le Maître A, Maillard E, Bourhis J; MACH-NC Collaborative Group. "Meta-analysis of chemotherapy in head and neck cancer (MACH-NC): an update on 93 randomised trials and 17,346 patients." *Radiother Oncol.* 2009;92(1):4–14. DOI: 10.1016/j.radonc.2009.04.014. PMID: 19446902.

**Extraction:**
- **Setting:** Individual patient data meta-analysis; definitive and postoperative settings; all chemotherapy approaches (concurrent, induction, adjuvant) vs. loco-regional treatment alone.
- **Cisplatin dose/schedule:** Heterogeneous across 93 trials. Concomitant chemotherapy shown to have the greatest benefit; single-agent cisplatin predominant.
- **Population:** 17,346 patients across 87 evaluable trials. All HNSCC subsites included (oral cavity, oropharynx, hypopharynx, larynx, nasopharynx). No OSCC-specific subgroup reported separately.
- **Key toxicity endpoints:** Not primary focus of this IPD meta-analysis; efficacy-focused.
- **Efficacy:** Overall: chemotherapy adds an absolute 4.5% survival benefit at 5 years (HR = 0.88; 95% CI: 0.85–0.92; P < 0.0001). Concomitant chemotherapy subgroup: absolute benefit 6.5% at 5 years (HR = 0.81; 95% CI: 0.76–0.86; P < 0.0001). Induction chemotherapy: smaller benefit (~2.4%); adjuvant chemotherapy: no significant benefit. Benefit increases with cisplatin-containing concomitant regimens.
- **Chronotherapy relevance:** The 6.5% absolute survival benefit from concomitant cisplatin is the strongest population-level justification for optimizing cisplatin delivery. The meta-analysis is agnostic to time of administration — reinforcing that the survival benefit was achieved entirely without circadian optimization, leaving open the possibility of further gains through chronotherapy.

### 12. NCCN Guidelines — Head and Neck Cancers (Version 2.2025)
**Citation:** National Comprehensive Cancer Network (NCCN). "NCCN Clinical Practice Guidelines in Oncology: Head and Neck Cancers." Version 2.2025. Available at: https://www.nccn.org/guidelines/guidelines-detail?category=1&id=1437 [accessed 2026-05-23].

**Extraction:**
- **Setting:** Comprehensive guideline covering definitive CRT, postoperative CRT, induction, and recurrent/metastatic settings for HNSCC including oral cavity SCC.
- **Cisplatin dose/schedule recommendations:**
  - Definitive concurrent CRT (preferred for locally advanced HNSCC): Cisplatin 100 mg/m² IV q3-weekly (days 1, 22, 43) — **Category 1** recommendation.
  - Alternative: Cisplatin 40 mg/m² IV weekly (acceptable when high-dose contraindicated).
  - Postoperative CRT (high-risk features — positive margins, ECE): Cisplatin 100 mg/m² q3-weekly — Category 1.
  - Induction: TPF (docetaxel 75 mg/m² d1, cisplatin 75–100 mg/m² d1, 5-FU 750–1000 mg/m²/day CI d1–4/5) — Category 2A for select patients.
  - Recurrent/Metastatic: Cisplatin or carboplatin + 5-FU ± cetuximab (EXTREME regimen) or pembrolizumab ± cisplatin/5-FU (KEYNOTE-048).
- **Population:** All HNSCC subsites; oral cavity SCC treated surgically first (preferred), with postoperative CRT for high-risk features.
- **Chronotherapy relevance:** No mention of time-of-day administration, chronotype, or circadian considerations in any version of the NCCN guidelines reviewed. The scheduling of cisplatin is entirely logistics-driven (RT slot availability, pharmacy mixing times, patient transportation). This is the clearest institutional confirmation that standard-of-care is biologically time-blind.

## Recurrent/Metastatic Setting

### 13. Vermorken JB, et al. (2008) — EXTREME Trial [note: not among primary 7 key papers but clinically essential]
**Citation:** Vermorken JB, Mesia R, Rivera F, et al. "Platinum-based chemotherapy plus cetuximab in head and neck cancer." *N Engl J Med.* 2008;359(11):1116–1127. DOI: 10.1056/NEJMoa0802656. PMID: 18784101.

**Extraction:**
- **Setting:** First-line recurrent/metastatic (R/M) HNSCC.
- **Cisplatin dose/schedule:** Cisplatin 100 mg/m² IV day 1 (or carboplatin AUC 2.5 days 1–3/4 weekly as cisplatin substitute) + 5-FU 1000 mg/m²/day CI days 1–4, q3-weekly x6 cycles ± cetuximab 400 mg/m² loading → 250 mg/m² weekly.
- **Population:** 442 patients (222 cetuximab + CT vs. 220 CT alone). R/M HNSCC — all subsites. About 21% oral cavity [verify].
- **Key toxicity endpoints:** Cetuximab arm: higher rates of acneiform rash, hypomagnesemia, infusion reactions. Platinum-related toxicity (renal, ototoxic, neuropathy) similar between arms. Grade 3–4 adverse events: 82% cetuximab arm vs. 76% CT alone.
- **Efficacy:** Median OS: 10.1 months (cetuximab + CT) vs. 7.4 months (CT alone); HR = 0.80; P = 0.04. First trial to show OS benefit by adding an agent to platinum doublet in R/M HNSCC.
- **Treatment completion:** Median cycles received: 6 (cetuximab arm) vs. 5 (CT alone).
- **Chronotherapy relevance:** R/M setting removes the daily RT logistical constraint on cisplatin timing. Cisplatin in q3-weekly cycles could theoretically be scheduled with circadian optimization (e.g., evening administration based on renal clock data), but no such approach has been tested in HNSCC R/M trials. This setting represents an accessible near-term opportunity for chronotherapy clinical trials.

## Additional Clinical Data: Nephrotoxicity and Completion Rates

**Key data points for review narrative (aggregate from searched literature):**

- High-dose cisplatin (100 mg/m²) q3-weekly during CRT: ~17% of patients unable to complete pre-planned courses due to nephrotoxicity (based on retrospective institutional series).
- Completion of all 3 planned q3-weekly cycles in CRT: as low as 49–64% in some series; CTCAE grade ≥3 nephrotoxicity 4–9% (varies by institutional hydration protocols and measurement timepoints).
- Weekly cisplatin (30–40 mg/m²): higher cycle completion (88% vs. 71% in Noronha 2018); grade ≥3 nephrotoxicity significantly lower.
- Acute kidney injury (AKI) during high-dose cisplatin CRT: 5.6% severe (grade III) in prospective series; magnesium supplementation emerging as nephroprotective strategy.
- **Scheduling reality:** In concurrent CRT, cisplatin administration is constrained to occur on the same days as RT — typically early in the morning — determined by radiation oncology department scheduling, pharmacy preparation lead times, IV chair availability, and patient transport logistics. There is no protocol-level consideration of patient chronotype, optimal cisplatin clock time, or circadian variation in renal detoxification or DNA repair. This institutional inertia is the primary structural barrier to cisplatin chronotherapy in HNSCC.
