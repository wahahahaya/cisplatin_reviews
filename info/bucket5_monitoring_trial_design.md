## Chronotype Questionnaires (MEQ, MCTQ)

### 1. Horne JA, Östberg O (1976)
**Full Citation:** Horne JA, Östberg O. A self-assessment questionnaire to determine morningness-eveningness in human circadian rhythms. *International Journal of Chronobiology*. 1976;4(2):97–110. PMID: 1027738.

- **Monitoring tool:** MEQ (Morningness-Eveningness Questionnaire) — 19-item self-report questionnaire; scores range 16–86; classifies five chronotypes from definitely morning to definitely evening.
- **Feasibility data:** Minimal patient burden; self-administered in ~5 minutes; no equipment required. Original validation in 18–32-year-old students; subsequently adapted for older and clinical populations.
- **Circadian biomarker measured:** Subjective chronotype preference; validated against oral temperature curves showing morning types have significantly earlier circadian temperature peaks. Proxy for suprachiasmatic nucleus phase.
- **Endpoint association:** Chronotype predicts preferred sleep/wake timing; moderate correlation with objective phase markers (DLMO, temperature acrophase). Increasing use in cancer risk epidemiology (evening types associated with higher breast cancer risk in UK Biobank data) but not yet validated as a predictor of chemotherapy toxicity or response in prospective trials.
- **Oncology setting:** General population instrument; limited direct oncology validation but widely used as baseline chronotype screen in clinical trials.
- **Validation status:** Extensively validated across cultures and age groups; considered gold-standard subjective chronotype measure. Correlates moderately with MCTQ (r ≈ 0.55–0.65).
- **Clinical utility:** Low burden, no cost, immediate result; appropriate as a first-line chronotype screen in HNSCC CRT trials. Cannot capture real-time circadian disruption during treatment.

### 2. Roenneberg T, Wirz-Justice A, Merrow M (2003)
**Full Citation:** Roenneberg T, Wirz-Justice A, Merrow M. Life between clocks: daily temporal patterns of human chronotypes. *Journal of Biological Rhythms*. 2003;18(1):80–90. DOI: 10.1177/0748730402239679. PMID: 12568247.

- **Monitoring tool:** MCTQ (Munich Chronotype Questionnaire) — self-report instrument quantifying sleep timing on work days vs. free days. Core metric: MSFsc (mid-sleep on free days, corrected for sleep debt).
- **Feasibility data:** Brief self-report; completed in ~5–10 minutes; no equipment; freely available. Large-scale validation: >500,000 participants in the MCTQ database (WorldWideSleep project).
- **Circadian biomarker measured:** MSFsc as a continuous quantitative proxy for circadian phase (later MSFsc = more evening-type). The MCTQ treats chronotype as a phase relationship between endogenous clock and zeitgeber cycle.
- **Endpoint association:** Epidemiological studies link evening chronotype (late MSFsc) with social jetlag, metabolic disruption, and cancer risk. No direct RCT data on chemotherapy toxicity stratification by MCTQ in oncology [verify for updated studies].
- **Oncology setting:** General population instrument; used in observational oncology studies (breast cancer risk). No HNSCC-specific data identified.
- **Validation status:** Highly validated; captures chronotype quantitatively rather than categorically; superior for research settings requiring continuous chronotype variables.
- **Clinical utility:** More granular than MEQ; preferred for research; MSFsc can be integrated with actigraphy data. Free days may be undefined for hospitalized/inpatient oncology patients — adaptation required for CRT setting.

## Actigraphy in Oncology

### 3. Mormont MC, Waterhouse J, Bleuzen P, et al. (2000)
**Full Citation:** Mormont MC, Waterhouse J, Bleuzen P, Giacchetti S, Jami A, Bogdan A, Lellouch J, Misset JL, Touitou Y, Lévi F. Marked 24-h rest/activity rhythms are associated with better quality of life, better response, and longer survival in patients with metastatic colorectal cancer and good performance status. *Clinical Cancer Research*. 2000;6(8):3038–3045. PMID: 10955782.

- **Monitoring tool:** Wrist actigraph (records accelerations per minute); worn for 3 days before chronomodulated chemotherapy. Two circadian metrics: (1) 24-h autocorrelation coefficient; (2) dichotomy index (I<O) — percentage of minutes in-bed with activity counts below median out-of-bed activity.
- **Feasibility data:** 200 patients enrolled; 192 with complete actigraphy data (96% completion). 3-day pre-treatment monitoring period; wristwatch-format device — low patient burden.
- **Circadian biomarker measured:** Amplitude and robustness of rest/activity rhythm (I<O dichotomy index as primary metric).
- **Endpoint association:** 2-year survival 5-fold higher in patients with marked vs. disrupted rest/activity rhythms. Robust rhythm independently associated with: better tumor response, better QoL (less fatigue), and longer survival in multivariate analysis. Strongest prognostic circadian actigraphy study in oncology.
- **Oncology setting:** Metastatic colorectal cancer (N=200); all receiving chronomodulated FOLFOX-type regimens.
- **Validation status:** Landmark validated study; actigraphy I<O metric subsequently adopted in multiple follow-up trials.
- **Clinical utility:** Foundational evidence that objective actigraphy-measured circadian robustness predicts oncology outcomes independently of performance status. Justifies pre-treatment actigraphy as a stratification biomarker in chronotherapy trials including HNSCC CRT.

### 4. Innominato PF, Giacchetti S, Bjarnason GA, et al. (2012)
**Full Citation:** Innominato PF, Giacchetti S, Bjarnason GA, Focan C, Garufi C, Coudert B, Iacobelli S, Tampellini M, Durando X, Mormont MC, Waterhouse J, Lévi FA. Prediction of overall survival through circadian rest-activity monitoring during chemotherapy for metastatic colorectal cancer. *International Journal of Cancer*. 2012;131(11):2684–2692. DOI: 10.1002/ijc.27574. PMID: 22488038.

- **Note:** The task description listed this as Cancer 118(13):3268–3275 PMID 22213170, but PMID 22213170 resolves to an unrelated hepatocarcinogenesis paper. The correct Innominato 2012 survival prediction paper is IJC 131(11):2684–92 PMID 22488038. A separate 2012 Cancer paper (fatigue/weight loss predictors) may exist — listed in the inaccessible file for verification.
- **Monitoring tool:** Wristwatch actigraph worn for 3 days during a chemotherapy course. I<O dichotomy index as primary circadian parameter (disruption defined as I<O ≤ 97.5%).
- **Feasibility data:** 77 patients from an international Phase III randomized trial (ARTDECO); actigraphy completed during chemotherapy — demonstrates feasibility during active treatment, not just pre-treatment.
- **Circadian biomarker measured:** I<O dichotomy index (disruption threshold ≤ 97.5%).
- **Endpoint association:** Circadian disruption occurred in 51% of patients. Disrupted patients had median OS 14.7 months vs. 22.3 months (HR 2.12, p = 0.004). Grade ≥2 fatigue 2-fold higher; weight loss ≥5% 3-fold higher in disrupted patients.
- **Oncology setting:** Metastatic colorectal cancer; first-line chronomodulated or conventional 5-FU/leucovorin/oxaliplatin.
- **Validation status:** Prospective data from Phase III trial — highest level of actigraphy evidence in oncology chronotherapy. Disruption confirmed as independent prognostic factor.
- **Clinical utility:** Establishes actigraphy monitoring during (not just before) chemotherapy as prognostically informative; identifies weight loss and fatigue as linked circadian disruption markers — relevant for HNSCC CRT monitoring protocols.

### 5. Cash E, Duck CR, Brinkman C, et al. (2018)
**Full Citation:** Cash E, Duck CR, Brinkman C, Rebholz W, Albert C, Worthen M, Jusufbegovic M, Wilson L, Bumpous JM. Depressive symptoms and actigraphy-measured circadian disruption predict head and neck cancer survival. *Psycho-Oncology*. 2018;27(10):2500–2507. DOI: 10.1002/pon.4862. PMID: 30117225.

- **Monitoring tool:** Wrist actigraph worn continuously for 6 days prior to chemoradiation; multiple circadian rest-activity parameters analyzed including nighttime restfulness.
- **Feasibility data:** 55 HNSCC patients; pre-treatment monitoring period; wearable actigraph — feasibility demonstrated in HNSCC population specifically.
- **Circadian biomarker measured:** Nighttime restfulness (strongest predictor), overall rest-activity rhythm parameters; PHQ-9 somatic depressive symptoms (co-predictor).
- **Endpoint association:** Nighttime restfulness HR = 0.910 (p = 0.009) for 2-year overall survival. Both circadian measures and somatic depression independently predicted shorter OS.
- **Oncology setting:** HNSCC specifically (N=55); assessed before chemoradiation — directly relevant to the manuscript topic.
- **Validation status:** Pilot/observational; first HNSCC-specific actigraphy circadian-survival study identified. Small sample limits generalizability; no chronotype questionnaire administered.
- **Clinical utility:** Critical evidence gap filler — demonstrates pre-treatment actigraphy is feasible in HNSCC CRT patients and predicts survival. Nighttime restfulness metric (simpler than I<O) shows strong effect. Must-cite for HNSCC-specific circadian monitoring rationale.

### 7. Ortiz-Tudela E, Innominato PF, Rol MA, Lévi F, Madrid JA (2016)
**Full Citation:** Ortiz-Tudela E, Innominato PF, Rol MA, Lévi F, Madrid JA. Relevance of internal time and circadian robustness for cancer patients. *BMC Cancer*. 2016;16:285. DOI: 10.1186/s12885-016-2319-9. PMID: 27102330. PMC: PMC4839139.

- **Monitoring tool:** TAP composite wrist sensor — records inner wrist surface Temperature, arm Activity, and Position (body posture) every 10 minutes continuously for 12 days (3 × 4-day periods: before/during/after chemotherapy). More informative than actigraphy alone as it adds temperature and posture masking correction.
- **Feasibility data:** 24 patients (advanced/metastatic GI cancer); 12-day continuous monitoring — demonstrates sustained wearable compliance across chemotherapy cycle. TAP sensor validated against actigraphy.
- **Circadian biomarker measured:** Individual circadian phase markers (M5 for temperature peak; L5 for activity nadir); Desynchronization Index (DI) — novel composite measure of inter-rhythm asynchrony. Phase differences of up to 12 hours among patients documented.
- **Endpoint association:** Chemotherapy produced modest circadian disruption followed by partial resynchronization. TAP + DI identified patient-specific timing windows for chronotherapy personalization.
- **Oncology setting:** Advanced/metastatic gastrointestinal cancer (N=24); chronomodulated chemotherapy.
- **Validation status:** Small pilot study; proof-of-concept for multi-parameter ambulatory monitoring. TAP composite measure validated as superior to actigraphy alone for phase estimation.
- **Clinical utility:** Up to 12-hour inter-individual phase differences justify individualized (not population-average) cisplatin timing. TAP monitoring with DI provides the richest circadian phenotyping available for chronotherapy trial stratification.

## DLMO and Melatonin Profiles in Cancer

### 8. Lewy AJ, Sack RL (1989)
**Full Citation:** Lewy AJ, Sack RL. The dim light melatonin onset as a marker for circadian phase position. *Chronobiology International*. 1989;6(1):93–102. DOI: 10.3109/07420528909059144. PMID: 2706705.

- **Monitoring tool:** DLMO protocol — sequential salivary or plasma melatonin sampling under controlled dim light conditions (<10 lux) beginning 6 hours before habitual sleep time; onset defined as rise above 3–10 pg/mL threshold.
- **Feasibility data:** Original protocol required controlled laboratory conditions; subsequent adaptations allow home salivary sampling with hourly collections. Patient burden: moderate (multiple timed saliva samples, dim light restriction). Collection kits mailed to patients are feasible in outpatient settings.
- **Circadian biomarker measured:** DLMO — the most accurate single marker of SCN-driven circadian phase in humans. Precedes habitual sleep onset by ~2 hours in normal adults. Sensitive to phase shifts from light, behavior, and drugs.
- **Endpoint association:** Foundational methodology paper — not an outcomes study. DLMO established as gold-standard reference against which MEQ, MCTQ, and actigraphy phase estimates are validated.
- **Oncology setting:** General/sleep medicine methodology; DLMO not yet applied routinely in oncology trials due to protocol complexity. Recommended as validation anchor in a small subset of chronotherapy trial participants.
- **Validation status:** Gold-standard validated circadian phase marker; 35+ years of subsequent validation studies. Concordance with MEQ/MCTQ moderate (r ≈ 0.5–0.6); concordance with TAP temperature phase higher.
- **Clinical utility:** DLMO is the objective phase truth standard. In an HNSCC CRT trial, DLMO could be performed in a subset (N=10–20) to validate actigraphy/MEQ phase estimates; impractical as a routine monitoring tool for all patients.

## Wearable Continuous Monitoring

### 10. Abusamak M, Abu-Samak A, Cai W, et al. (2025)
**Full Citation:** Abusamak M, Abu-Samak A, Cai W, Al-Waeli H, Al-Hamed FS, Al-Tamimi M, Juweid M, Chaurasia A, Nicolau B, Tamimi F. Chronotherapy in head and neck cancer: A systematic review and meta-analysis. *International Journal of Cancer*. 2025;156(5):1015–1032. DOI: 10.1002/ijc.35234. PMID: 39508699.

- **Monitoring tool:** Systematic review of timing-based interventions; individual included trials used varying schedules (not standardized circadian monitoring). No unified chronotype assessment tool identified across reviewed trials.
- **Feasibility data:** Review of multiple trials; standardized circadian monitoring was absent in most included studies — identified as a key methodological gap.
- **Circadian biomarker measured:** None standardized across studies; chrono-timing based on clock time rather than individual circadian phase.
- **Endpoint association:** Morning radiotherapy vs. evening: 31% less risk of grade ≥3 mucositis. Platinum-based chrono-chemotherapy: 73% less risk of lower ORR; 41% less overall toxicity vs. conventional scheduling.
- **Oncology setting:** HNSCC specifically — cisplatin-based chronomodulated chemotherapy and chrono-radiotherapy data synthesized.
- **Validation status:** Systematic review and meta-analysis (highest evidence level for the question); published in International Journal of Cancer (high-quality journal).
- **Clinical utility:** Provides strongest current evidence for chronotherapy benefit in HNSCC. The lack of individual circadian monitoring across reviewed trials is the key trial design gap this manuscript should address. Establishes clinical need for chronotype-stratified HNSCC CRT trials.

## Trial Design Lessons for Chronotherapy

### 15. Levi F et al. (2003 — Lancet Oncology) / Systematic Review Insights
**Key design principle papers:**

**Bjarnason GA, Jordan RCK (2002):** Circadian variation of cell proliferation and its relevance to cancer chronotherapy. [verify journal — likely *In Vivo* or Cancer Lett; covers oral mucosa and tumor proliferation circadian rhythms — directly relevant to HNSCC]

**Abusamak et al. 2025 (IJC meta-analysis, cited above):** Identifies key trial design deficiency — absence of individual chronotype assessment in all reviewed HNSCC chronotherapy trials. Recommends circadian-based multicentric optimized studies.

**Ortiz-Tudela et al. 2016 (BMC Cancer, cited above):** Documents up to 12-hour inter-patient circadian variation — implies population-average timing is insufficient; stratification by individual phase essential.

**Lévi F et al. 2020 (Cancers, cited above):** Tele-monitoring model demonstrates feasibility of remote real-time circadian surveillance in multicenter oncology trials.

**Key design recommendations synthesized from literature:**
1. Baseline chronotype assessment (MEQ or MCTQ) as minimal screen for all patients.
2. Pre-treatment 3–7-day actigraphy to establish individual I<O, IS, IV, L5, M10.
3. TAP or equivalent multi-parameter monitoring (activity + temperature + light) during CRT cycles.
4. DLMO in a validation subset (N=15–20) to anchor phase estimates.
5. Repeat actigraphy mid-treatment to detect CRT-induced circadian disruption.
6. Document dexamethasone timing and dose relative to circadian phase.
7. Stratify randomization (in future RCT) by baseline circadian robustness (disrupted vs. intact rhythm).
8. Pre-specify circadian disruption as an adverse event endpoint (not just a covariate).
