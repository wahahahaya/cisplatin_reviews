## CTCAE Grading Tool References

### Paper B1 — CTCAE v3.0 Development Paper

**Citation:** Trotti A, Colevas AD, Setser A, Rusch V, Jaques D, Budach V, Langer C, Murphy B, Cumberlin R, Coleman CN, Rubin P. CTCAE v3.0: development of a comprehensive grading system for the adverse effects of cancer treatment. *Seminars in Radiation Oncology*. 2003;13(3):176–181. DOI: 10.1053/s1053-4296(03)00031-6. PMID: 12903007.

**Toxicity domain:** All domains — universal grading framework

**Clinical tool:** Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events (CTCAE) v3.0; first multimodality, comprehensive AE grading system. Grades 1–5 with standardized severity descriptors across all organ systems.

**Key finding:** CTCAE v3.0 represented the first internationally consensus-validated, multimodality system for grading acute and late effects of cancer treatment, enabling cross-trial AE comparability. It introduced new guidelines for late effects, surgical, pediatric, and multimodality AE reporting.

**Relevance to chronotherapy trial design:** CTCAE grades are the universal language for reporting toxicity endpoints. Using CTCAE ≥ Grade 3 as a primary endpoint for cisplatin chronotherapy trials directly operationalizes the key hypothesis that time-of-day dosing reduces clinically significant (i.e., grade ≥ 3) toxicity burden. Provides the measurement framework for all toxicity endpoints across nephrotoxicity, ototoxicity, mucositis, neurotoxicity, and emesis domains.

### Paper B2 — NCI CTCAE v5.0 (Institutional Reference)

**Citation:** National Cancer Institute. Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events (CTCAE), Version 5.0. Published: November 27, 2017. U.S. Department of Health and Human Services. Available at: https://ctep.cancer.gov/protocoldevelopment/electronic_applications/ctc.htm

**Toxicity domain:** All domains — current operational standard

**Clinical tool:** CTCAE v5.0; current standard for clinical trial adverse event reporting globally. Five-grade system; domains include nephrotoxicity (creatinine elevation), hearing loss, nausea/vomiting, mucositis, peripheral neuropathy.

**Key finding:** CTCAE v5.0 supersedes v3.0 as the operationally required grading tool for NCI-sponsored and most international trials as of 2017. Provides the specific grade thresholds (e.g., Grade 3 hearing loss = "audiometric testing required; intervention indicated") that would define primary endpoints in cisplatin chronotherapy trials.

**Relevance to chronotherapy trial design:** Any proposed chronotherapy RCT submitted for regulatory approval would use CTCAE v5.0 grading. Documenting incidence of CTCAE Grade ≥ 3 events as primary endpoints (rather than survival) is the regulatory-consistent approach for a toxicity-reduction trial. CTCAE v5.0 is therefore the definitional backbone for all toxicity-first endpoint justifications.

## CINV Prevention (Antiemetic Guidelines)

### Paper B3 — ASCO Antiemetics Guideline 2017

**Citation:** Hesketh PJ, Kris MG, Basch E, Bohlke K, Barbour SY, Clark-Snow RA, Danso MA, Dennis K, Dupuis LL, Dusetzina SB, Eng C, Feyer PC, Jordan K, Noonan K, Sparacio D, Somerfield MR, Lyman GH. Antiemetics: American Society of Clinical Oncology Clinical Practice Guideline Update. *Journal of Clinical Oncology*. 2017;35(28):3240–3261. DOI: 10.1200/JCO.2017.74.4789. PMID: 28759346.

**Toxicity domain:** Chemotherapy-induced nausea and vomiting (CINV)

**Clinical tool / intervention:** Guideline recommends four-drug antiemetic prophylaxis for high emetic risk (HER) chemotherapy including cisplatin: NK1 receptor antagonist + 5-HT3 receptor antagonist + dexamethasone + olanzapine on day 1, continued with dexamethasone and olanzapine on days 2–4. Based on systematic review of 41 publications (2009–2016).

**Key finding:** Adding olanzapine to the three-drug NK1/5-HT3/dexamethasone backbone significantly reduces nausea incidence in patients receiving HER chemotherapy. Cisplatin is classified as a high emetic risk agent. Without prophylaxis, acute vomiting occurs in >90% of patients receiving cisplatin.

**Relevance to chronotherapy trial design:** CINV is a primary quality-of-life toxicity with cisplatin. Even with optimal four-drug prophylaxis per this ASCO guideline, delayed nausea (days 2–5) remains poorly controlled in a significant proportion of patients. Chronotherapy evidence suggests evening cisplatin administration may reduce CINV severity. This guideline establishes the current antiemetic standard of care against which chronotherapy CINV improvement would be measured, and justifies CINV control rate as a secondary endpoint.

**Journal tier:** Journal of Clinical Oncology — flagship ASCO journal, impact factor ~45.

### Paper B4 — MASCC/ESMO CINV Guideline 2016

**Citation:** Roila F, Molassiotis A, Herrstedt J, Aapro M, Gralla RJ, Bruera E, Clark-Snow RA, Dupuis LL, Einhorn LH, Feyer P, Hesketh PJ, Jordan K, Olver I, Rapoport BL, Roscoe J, Sanna T, Schrijvers D, Som T, Warr D, Warr D; participants of the MASCC/ESMO Consensus Conference Copenhagen 2015. 2016 MASCC and ESMO guideline update for the prevention of chemotherapy- and radiotherapy-induced nausea and vomiting and of nausea and vomiting in advanced cancer patients. *Annals of Oncology*. 2016;27(suppl 5):v119–v133. DOI: 10.1093/annonc/mdw270. PMID: 27664248.

**Toxicity domain:** CINV — includes radiotherapy-induced nausea and vomiting (RINV), highly relevant for concurrent chemoradiotherapy (CRT) in HNSCC

**Clinical tool / intervention:** International consensus guideline (Copenhagen 2015 conference) recommending 5-HT3 antagonist + dexamethasone + NK1 antagonist for HER agents including cisplatin. Includes specific guidance for moderately emetogenic chemotherapy (MEC), radiotherapy-combined protocols, and advanced cancer settings.

**Key finding:** For cisplatin-based HER regimens, three-drug prophylaxis (aprepitant/fosaprepitant + ondansetron + dexamethasone) is the minimum standard. The guideline explicitly addresses concurrent chemoradiotherapy, noting that combined modality treatment increases CINV risk above that of either modality alone — directly applicable to HNSCC CRT.

**Relevance to chronotherapy trial design:** HNSCC patients receiving concurrent cisplatin-radiotherapy experience compounded CINV from both modalities. This guideline provides the European/international standard of care context. A chronotherapy trial in HNSCC would need to demonstrate CINV outcomes measured against this standard. The guideline also supports CINV severity as a key patient-reported outcome, justifying its inclusion as a secondary toxicity endpoint.

**Journal tier:** Annals of Oncology — top-tier oncology journal, IF ~51.

## Nephroprotection Strategies

### Paper B5 — Systematic Review of Cisplatin Nephroprotection Strategies

**Citation:** Crona DJ, Faso A, Nishijima TF, McGraw KA, Galsky MD, Milowsky MI. A Systematic Review of Strategies to Prevent Cisplatin-Induced Nephrotoxicity. *The Oncologist*. 2017;22(5):609–619. DOI: 10.1634/theoncologist.2016-0319. PMID: 28438887.

**Toxicity domain:** Nephrotoxicity

**Clinical tool / intervention:** Systematic review of 24 clinical studies evaluating: (1) vigorous IV hydration; (2) magnesium supplementation (8–16 mEq IV); (3) mannitol forced diuresis; (4) amifostine; (5) short-duration outpatient hydration protocols.

**Key finding:** Hydration is essential for all patients. Short-duration, low-volume outpatient hydration is safe for intermediate to high-dose cisplatin. IV magnesium supplementation (8–16 mEq) shows nephroprotective benefit. Amifostine has modest nephroprotective evidence but clinical adoption is limited by side effects. No nephroprotective drug has achieved universal standard-of-care status beyond hydration.

**Relevance to chronotherapy trial design:** Cisplatin nephrotoxicity (CTCAE creatinine elevation, GFR decline) is a dose-limiting toxicity that causes treatment delays, dose reductions, and permanent renal impairment. Chronotherapy evidence suggests cisplatin nephrotoxicity follows circadian patterns, with renal tubular DNA repair and cisplatin efflux transporter expression varying by time of day. This systematic review establishes that current nephroprotection is incomplete, strengthening the rationale for chronotherapy as an additive strategy. Renal function preservation (serum creatinine, GFR) is a standard secondary endpoint in cisplatin trials.

**Journal tier:** The Oncologist — peer-reviewed ASCO journal, IF ~6.

### Paper B6 — Magnesium Supplementation and Risk Factors for Cisplatin Nephrotoxicity

**Citation:** Kidera Y, Kawakami H, Sakiyama T, Okamoto K, Tanaka K, Takeda M, Kaneda H, Nishina S, Tsuya A, Tomiyasu H, Yoshimoto N, Matsuoka H, Katayama K, Kimura T, Ogata Y, Ueda S, Mori M, Miyazaki Y, Naito Y, Tsurutani J, Nakagawa K. Risk factors for cisplatin-induced nephrotoxicity and potential of magnesium supplementation for renal protection. *PLoS One*. 2014;9(7):e101902. DOI: 10.1371/journal.pone.0101902. PMID: 25020203.

**Toxicity domain:** Nephrotoxicity

**Clinical tool / intervention:** Retrospective cohort study (n=401 patients); IV magnesium supplementation assessed as a nephroprotective intervention. Risk ratio analysis for multiple clinical factors.

**Key finding:** IV magnesium supplementation was associated with a significantly reduced risk of cisplatin-induced nephrotoxicity (risk ratio 0.175; p=0.0004). Low magnesium levels during treatment were significantly associated with greater serum creatinine elevation. ECOG PS ≥ 2 and NSAID use were independent risk factors.

**Relevance to chronotherapy trial design:** Documents the quantitative magnitude of magnesium's nephroprotective effect in real-world clinical practice. Establishes a key baseline intervention that would need to be standardized (and controlled for) in any chronotherapy trial examining cisplatin nephrotoxicity as a primary endpoint. Relevant for trial stratification by magnesium use status.

**Journal tier:** PLoS One — open access mega-journal. Note: acceptable for epidemiological data; prefer to cite alongside Crona 2017 (The Oncologist) for the nephroprotection evidence base.

## Ototoxicity Prevention

### Paper B7 — Sodium Thiosulfate for Cisplatin Ototoxicity (Children) — Brock 2018 NEJM

**Citation:** Brock PR, Maibach R, Childs M, Rajput K, Roebuck D, Sullivan MJ, Laithier V, Ronghe M, Dall'Igna P, Haber M, Doz F, Cohn SL, Nicholson JC, Harms D, Frühwald MC, Gentet JC, Kohler B, Ellison D, Michalski A, Grundy R, Routh C, Saran F, Dommett R, Chastagner P, Gaze M, Michalak C, Morland B, Pizer B, Squire R, Lobel U, Gruhn B, Lode HN, Stojanovic-Rundic S, Eggen B, Bhatt N, Gerber NU, Berthold F, Fleischhack G, von Schweinitz D, Castleberry R, Owens C, Vora A, Kohler J, Smith VR, Dorman MF, Okonkwo U, Thomas B, Barry MB, Landes C, Bode U, Kretschmar C; SIOPEL group. Sodium Thiosulfate for Protection from Cisplatin-Induced Hearing Loss. *New England Journal of Medicine*. 2018;378(25):2376–2385. DOI: 10.1056/NEJMoa1801109. PMID: 29924955.

**Toxicity domain:** Ototoxicity (sensorineural hearing loss)

**Clinical tool / intervention:** Phase 3 randomized controlled trial (SIOPEL-6); sodium thiosulfate (STS) administered IV 6 hours after cisplatin in pediatric hepatoblastoma. STS acts as a cisplatin-scavenging agent in cochlear tissue.

**Key finding:** STS reduced cisplatin-induced hearing loss by 48%: hearing loss of Brock grade ≥ 1 occurred in 63% without STS vs. 33% with STS (p<0.001). Crucially, no difference in event-free or overall survival was observed, establishing STS does not compromise antitumor efficacy. This is the landmark RCT establishing STS efficacy.

**Relevance to chronotherapy trial design:** Establishes proof of concept that organ-specific protection from cisplatin ototoxicity is achievable without compromising oncological outcomes — the exact same logic underlying chronotherapy. This trial is the strongest analog for a toxicity-first endpoint trial: survival was not the primary endpoint; ototoxicity reduction was. Directly supports using CTCAE ≥ Grade 2 hearing loss as a primary endpoint in chronotherapy trials. Also relevant because ototoxicity in adult HNSCC patients (who may already have age-related hearing loss) constitutes a major QoL burden.

**Journal tier:** New England Journal of Medicine — highest impact medical journal.

## Mucositis in HNC Chemoradiotherapy (Incidence / Severity)

### Paper B9 — MASCC/ISOO Mucositis Clinical Practice Guidelines

**Citation:** Lalla RV, Bowen J, Barasch A, Elting L, Epstein J, Keefe DM, McGuire DB, Migliorati C, Nicolatou-Galitis O, Peterson DE, Raber-Durlacher JE, Sonis ST, Elad S; Mucositis Guidelines Leadership Group of the Multinational Association of Supportive Care in Cancer and International Society of Oral Oncology (MASCC/ISOO). MASCC/ISOO clinical practice guidelines for the management of mucositis secondary to cancer therapy. *Cancer*. 2014;120(10):1453–1461. DOI: 10.1002/cncr.28592. PMID: 24615748.

**Toxicity domain:** Oral and gastrointestinal mucositis

**Clinical tool / intervention:** Evidence-based clinical practice guidelines. 32 recommendations developed from systematic review of >570 studies (>8,000 initial papers screened). Interventions covered: oral hygiene, low-level light therapy (LLLT), cryotherapy, keratinocyte growth factor, amifostine, antimicrobial agents, analgesics, mucosal barrier agents.

**Key finding:** LLLT and cryotherapy have evidence of benefit in specific settings. Basic oral hygiene is universally recommended. Mucositis is classified as a highly significant dose-limiting toxicity. The guidelines explicitly note that oral mucositis remains inadequately managed despite multiple interventional trials, particularly in the head and neck chemoradiotherapy setting.

**Relevance to chronotherapy trial design:** This guideline defines the current clinical standard of care for mucositis management. In HNSCC patients undergoing cisplatin-based CRT, oral mucositis (OM) is a near-universal toxicity (incidence ~80–91%; Grade 3–4 in 60–70%) that causes treatment interruptions, hospitalization, feeding tube dependence, and significant QoL impairment. Mucositis reduction is therefore a high-value endpoint for any chronotherapy CRT trial in HNSCC. This guideline provides the management framework against which chronotherapy effects on mucositis should be measured.

**Journal tier:** Cancer (American Cancer Society) — major oncology journal, IF ~6.

### Paper B10 — Mucositis Incidence and Severity in HNC: Systematic Literature Review

**Citation:** Trotti A, Bellm LA, Epstein JB, Frame D, Fuchs HJ, Gwede CK, Komaroff E, Nalysnyk L, Zilberberg MD. Mucositis incidence, severity and associated outcomes in patients with head and neck cancer receiving radiotherapy with or without chemotherapy: a systematic literature review. *Radiotherapy and Oncology*. 2003;66(3):253–262. DOI: 10.1016/s0167-8140(02)00404-8. PMID: 12742264.

**Toxicity domain:** Oral mucositis — clinical burden in HNSCC

**Clinical tool / intervention:** Systematic literature review (33 studies, n=6,181 patients). Assessed mucositis incidence, severity, and associated clinical outcomes (hospitalization, treatment interruptions, nutritional compromise).

**Key finding:** Mean mucositis incidence was 80%. Grade 3–4 mucositis occurred in 56% of patients receiving altered fractionation RT vs. 34% with conventional RT. Hospitalization due to mucositis: 16% overall, rising to 32% for altered fractionation patients. Treatment interruption/modification due to mucositis: ~11% of patients. Grade 3–4 mucositis was more frequent in oropharynx/oral cavity primaries receiving concurrent chemotherapy.

**Relevance to chronotherapy trial design:** This is the foundational systematic review establishing the quantitative clinical burden of mucositis in HNSCC chemoradiotherapy — the precise patient population targeted by cisplatin chronotherapy trials. The data directly supports mucositis (CTCAE Grade ≥ 3) as a clinically meaningful primary endpoint: it occurs in the majority of patients, causes hospitalizations and treatment interruptions, and has no pharmacological prophylaxis equivalent to antiemetic or otoprotective regimens. Chronotherapy targeting mucositis reduction could meaningfully reduce treatment interruption rates and hospitalization costs.

**Journal tier:** Radiotherapy and Oncology — leading international radiotherapy journal, IF ~5.5.

## Peripheral Neuropathy Clinical Burden

### Paper B11 — Cavaletti & Marmiroli: Chemotherapy-Induced Peripheral Neurotoxicity

**Citation:** Cavaletti G, Marmiroli P. Chemotherapy-induced peripheral neurotoxicity. *Nature Reviews Neurology*. 2010;6(12):657–666. DOI: 10.1038/nrneurol.2010.160. PMID: 21060341.

**Toxicity domain:** Peripheral neuropathy (chemotherapy-induced peripheral neurotoxicity, CIPN)

**Clinical tool / intervention:** Comprehensive review of CIPN mechanisms, clinical manifestations, dose-response relationships, assessment tools, and neuroprotective strategies. Covers cisplatin, oxaliplatin, taxanes, and vinca alkaloids. Assessment tools reviewed include Total Neuropathy Score (TNS), patient-reported outcome instruments, and NCI-CTCAE grading for neuropathy.

**Key finding:** CIPN is a common, potentially severe, dose-limiting adverse effect of cancer treatment. Cisplatin produces predominantly sensory neuropathy (dorsal root ganglion neurons are primary targets) with cumulative dose-dependence and potential for permanence ("coasting" phenomenon). CIPN significantly impairs daily activities, balance, and fine motor function, with QoL impact persisting after treatment completion. No neuroprotective agent has achieved standard-of-care approval for CIPN prevention.

**Relevance to chronotherapy trial design:** Cisplatin-induced neuropathy is cumulative and irreversible — making prevention superior to treatment. Cisplatin neuropathy risk in HNSCC patients (particularly older adults who may receive high cumulative doses across multiple cycles) is a major chronic QoL burden. Circadian variation in DNA repair and platinum uptake in dorsal root ganglia neurons is biologically plausible as a chronotherapy target. This review justifies peripheral neuropathy (CTCAE Grade ≥ 2 sensory neuropathy) as a clinically meaningful secondary endpoint in chronotherapy trials, particularly for patients receiving ≥3 cycles of high-dose cisplatin CRT.

**Journal tier:** Nature Reviews Neurology — top-tier systematic review journal, IF ~34.
